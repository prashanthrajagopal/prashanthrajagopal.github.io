<p class="rt-summary">Two Kerala hill stations from the reels, joined by the roads nobody uses. Nelliyampathy first — the underrated one, a 15 km ghat above Pothundi dam that ends at the Seetharkundu cliff and the forest department&#x27;s jeep tracks. Then instead of the Kochi highway, Munnar from the back: Udumalpet through Chinnar sanctuary to Marayoor&#x27;s sandalwood forest and up the Kanthalloor side. The Anachal glass bridge — shut on its opening day in Dec 2025 and reopened by court order in July 2026 — is 12 km out of Munnar; after it, the reel says, &#x27;the real thrill begins&#x27;: the steep estate road below Anachal. Leave Munnar east over the Bodimettu ghat into Tamil Nadu and run home through Theni and Dindigul.</p>
<p class="rt-credit">Route idea: @sherry_thegirlnextdoor (Nelliyampathy) &amp; @the_pretty_escape.travel (Munnar glass bridge) on Instagram — two reels; the back roads between them are the point of this loop</p>

<div><div class="rt-panel "><h3>Highlights</h3><ul><li>Seetharkundu — the cliff-edge viewpoint at Nelliyampathy, 1,000 ft straight down to the Palakkad plains, usually with a waterfall in view.</li><li>Nelliyampathy&#x27;s forest jeep tracks to Mampara — wild elephants at dawn, bookable at Kaikatty (₹50 entry + jeep).</li><li>The Chinnar–Marayoor road — dry-forest sanctuary, sandalwood, and the megalithic dolmens above Marayoor.</li><li>The Anachal glass bridge — and, more to the point, the estate road below it.</li><li>Bodimettu ghat — Munnar&#x27;s quiet eastern exit, hairpins through shola forest to the plains with almost no tourist traffic.</li></ul></div><div class="rt-panel rt-offroad"><h3>Off-road &amp; motorcycling notes</h3><div class="rt-rating" aria-label="Off-road rating 2 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class=""></i><i class=""></i><i class=""></i></div><p class="rt-lead">Tarmac throughout, but three of the legs are narrow forest or estate roads where surface and wildlife matter more than speed. Any bike handles it; an ADV is more comfortable on the broken Nelliyampathy and Marayoor stretches.</p><ul><li>Nelliyampathy ghat: narrow, with monsoon-broken edges and no railings at the falls. No fuel or food on the hill — Nenmara is the last.</li><li>Chinnar sanctuary road: single lane, elephants and gaur; check post allows daytime only. Don&#x27;t stop for photos where there&#x27;s no shoulder.</li><li>Anachal estate road: steep enough that the reel&#x27;s commenters &#x27;smell clutch plates&#x27; — first gear, and check the brakes before the descent.</li><li>Bodimettu ghat: cold and foggy early morning; the Tamil Nadu side has broken patches and truck traffic near Bodi.</li><li>Leech socks in the bag if any of it is within a month of the monsoon.</li></ul></div></div>
<div><div class="rt-panel rt-timings"><h3>Timings, permits &amp; fuel</h3><ul><li>Pothundi dam: 8 AM – 6 PM, ₹10. Kaikatty Forest IB (jeep tracks, Mampara): 7 AM – 3 PM; ₹50 forest entry, jeeps ₹1,200–2,000.</li><li>Seetharkundu viewpoint: 6 AM – 6 PM, free.</li><li>Chinnar Wildlife Sanctuary road (Udumalpet–Marayoor): daytime crossing only; check-post timings roughly 6 AM – 6 PM.</li><li>Anachal glass bridge reopened July 2026 after a court order; hours and price change — check before relying on it.</li><li>Fuel: Coimbatore, Palakkad, Nenmara, Pollachi, Udumalpet, Marayoor, Munnar, Bodinayakanur, Theni. Fill at Nenmara and at Udumalpet.</li><li>Stays: Coimbatore (night 1), Pollachi or Nelliyampathy estate bungalows (night 2), Munnar (night 3).</li></ul></div><div class="rt-panel rt-caution"><h3>Cautions</h3><ul><li>Nelliyampathy is best avoided Jun–Aug (landslides, closed jeep tracks). Munnar is fine in monsoon but the Anachal road isn&#x27;t.</li><li>The glass bridge was built on disputed land and closed once already — treat it as a bonus, not the reason for the trip.</li><li>Wild elephants: Chinnar, the Marayoor–Munnar road at dusk, and the Bodimettu ghat. Give way, engine off.</li><li>Four long days; the Bangalore–Coimbatore and Dindigul–Bangalore legs are 365 and 425 km of highway — don&#x27;t stack them with the hill days.</li></ul></div></div>
